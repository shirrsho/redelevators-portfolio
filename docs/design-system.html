04 — Foundations

Flat at rest. Shadow is a response.

Hairline borders define edges, ground-tone changes separate regions, and shadow arrives when the cursor does. If every card already floats, the lift has nothing to say. Exceptions are allowed where a surface genuinely hovers above the page or is the single most important target — argued from the element’s role, never applied to a grid by default.

Soft

Scrolled nav bar, testimonial cards, floating chips. Nearly subliminal.

05 — Foundations

Motion is the argument, not the decoration.

The proof that these systems run without you is that the page itself is visibly running. A page that sits perfectly still is off-brand in a way no color or type choice can rescue — on surfaces that persuade. In reading contexts, motion recedes to state feedback only.

Standard easing

cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)

Fast out, long settle. Every entrance, draw-on and state transition.

Durations
  • 700ms · entrance reveal, 24px rise + fade
  • 300ms · hover and focus transitions
  • 80ms · stagger between siblings
  • 1400ms · counter count-up
  • 16s / 32s · aurora drift / marquee

Card lift is a spring: stiffness 300, damping 22. That 1.03 / 0.95 hover-and-press pair is the system’s tactile signature.

Reduced motion

Honored in two layers, and both must be maintained. The global CSS override collapses all animation and transition durations; components additionally check useReducedMotion() to skip transforms and render final states directly. The CSS alone does not stop a JS-driven counter or a keyframed transform loop. This page obeys the same rule — every animation on it stops under a reduced-motion preference.

08 — Doctrine

Sixteen rules, so the next page does not need a meeting.

Named rules travel further than bullet lists. When something new comes up that no rule covers, reason from the North Star.

ColorThe Single Voice Rule

Elevator Red is the only chromatic color. When a surface seems to need a second, the answer is a neutral, a tone of red, or a change of ground. A second hue is a redesign, not an addition.

ColorThe Signal Rule

Red marks what is live, what is next, or what is the answer. It never decorates.

ColorThe Ground Change Rule

Sections separate by changing ground — Paper, Chalk, Graphite, red panel — not by stacking dividers.

ColorThe Light-Only Rule

No dark theme, no prefers-color-scheme handling, no toggle. The unused dark brand assets are out of scope, not a defect. Provisional, by decision.

ColorThe Contrast Floor Rule

Every text colour has a measured ratio against its ground. Neutrals pass AA. Elevator Red does not pass as small text — 3.69:1 on Paper — so red below 24px is a known open failure awaiting a brand decision. Never set a text colour by eye, and flatten any opacity before measuring.

TypographyThe Three Voices Rule

Space Grotesk states, Inter explains, JetBrains Mono labels. The jobs never swap.

TypographyThe Tight Top Rule

Headings run at -0.03em and line-height 1.02. Body inherits none of it and stays at 1.6.

TypographyThe Unlayered Heading Rule

The site’s globals.css styles headings outside any cascade layer, so they beat every Tailwind utility. A typography class on a heading is silently discarded — verify in the browser until the layer fix lands.

TypographyThe Label-First Rule

Every section opens with a mono label above its headline. A heading without one is incomplete.

LayoutThe One Column Rule

Everything lives in the same 1152px container with a 24px gutter. Backgrounds may go full-bleed; content may not.

ElevationThe Flat-Until-Touched Rule

Cards rest flat and gain the Lift shadow on hover. Exceptions must be argued from the element’s role, not applied to a grid.

ElevationThe Untinted Shadow Rule

Shadows are neutral Graphite at low alpha. The primary button owns the only colored glow.

ShapesThe Radius Ladder Rule

12px controls, 16px cards, 24px large cards, 32px panels, full for pills. The arc matches the edge it turns.

ShapesThe One Hairline Rule

One border color on light grounds, at one width. If an edge needs presence, change the ground behind it.

ComponentsThe Reading Calm Rule

Ambient motion belongs to surfaces that persuade. In articles and long-form prose it recedes to hover, focus and press. Reveal-on-scroll down 2,000 words is an obstacle, not a signature.

ComponentsThe Visible Focus Rule

Every interactive element carries a focus-visible treatment that survives all four grounds. Nothing may rely on the browser default, and nothing may remove the outline without replacing it.

09 — Guardrails

What we do, and what we refuse.

The refusals matter more than the prescriptions. Three named anti-references keep this site from drifting into the three looks every agency site drifts into.

Do
  • Open every section with a mono label above the headline, and reuse the section-heading composition rather than rebuilding it.
  • Keep content inside the 1152px container; let only backgrounds go full-bleed.
  • Let cards rest flat and earn their shadow on hover — and where a surface genuinely floats or is the page’s single most important target, give it a resting shadow deliberately.
  • Scale radius to the element: 12px controls, 16px cards, 24px large cards, 32px panels.
  • Make new sections move. Ambient motion is the system’s core argument.
  • Honor reduced motion in both layers — the global CSS override and the component-level check.
  • Keep the mark’s clear space equal to one bar’s width on all sides.
  • Give every interactive element a focus treatment that survives Paper, Chalk, Graphite and the red panel.
  • Prove capability by showing mechanism — the real chain, the real tools — rather than by asserting an outcome.
Don’t
  • Introduce a second chromatic color. One red, everything else neutral.
  • Use red where nothing is live, next, or being asked of the visitor.
  • Add dark mode, theme handling, or a toggle.
  • Drift toward the generic AI-startup look: purple and blue gradients, frosted glass, dark-by-default surfaces, floating 3D shapes, glowing orbs. The likeliest failure mode for an automation agency.
  • Drift toward the corporate stock-photo agency look: meeting rooms, handshakes, corporate blue, abstract “solutions” language.
  • Drift toward loud growth-hacker marketing: countdown timers, scarcity badges, neon slabs, urgency the product does not have.
  • Swap the type roles — no mono body copy, no Inter headlines, no Space Grotesk paragraphs.
  • Add a second border color, or a border heavier than 1px.
  • Tint a shadow. The primary button owns the only colored glow.
  • Invent a number, client, logo, testimonial or date. Not as a placeholder, not as lorem, not “to swap later.”
  • Design a composition that only holds together when filled with metrics or testimonials. A section must read as finished with real content, or with the slot honestly empty.
  • Invent a second proof device. If a surface needs credibility, it gets a System Anatomy.

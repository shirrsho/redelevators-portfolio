Design review · not part of the public site

Nav & hero

The two things every visitor sees first. The anatomy, every state, and the real pages at desktop, tablet and phone width. The frames are live, so you can scroll and click inside them.

01 · NAV ANATOMY · DESKTOP

A floating bar: logo, five links, one action.

A rounded bar that floats 14px below the top, lined up with the page content. Links sit in the exact centre. Nothing else competes with “Book a call”.

02 · NAV STATES

Always there, firmer on scroll.

At the top it’s a light glass bar over the hero. Once you scroll, the glass gets darker and the edge a little brighter, so it stays readable over any content.

03 · NAV ON MOBILE (UNDER 900PX)

Logo, Book, menu. That’s it.

The same floating bar, 10px from the edges. “Book a call” stays visible next to the menu button, so the main action is never hidden. The menu opens a full-screen sheet: big links with a one-line hint each, then the booking button and email at the bottom.

09 · HERO RULES

Same rules on every page.

One H1, two lines maxShort enough to read in one glance at phone width.
One line of supportThe lead says who it’s for or what happens next. Never a paragraph.
Red = bookThe only red button on screen is always the booking action.
The visual movesEvery hero visual shows something working, not a stock image.
First screen fitsHeadline and primary button are visible without scrolling at 375×667.
Same eyebrowSmall label above the H1 naming the page or the audience.
